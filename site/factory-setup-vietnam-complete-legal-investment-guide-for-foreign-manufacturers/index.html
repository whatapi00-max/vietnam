Factory Setup Vietnam: Complete Legal & Investment Guide for Foreign Manufacturers

Summary

Vietnam has become one of Southeast Asia’s leading manufacturing destinations for foreign investors seeking supply chain diversification, industrial scalability, and long-term access to Asian markets. However, factory setup Vietnam involves far more than company incorporation. Successful manufacturing investment requires careful navigation of investment licensing, industrial land regulations, environmental approvals, labor compliance, customs procedures, tax structuring, and post-licensing execution. This guide explains the legal and operational framework for establishing manufacturing operations in Vietnam.

Why Foreign Manufacturers Are Expanding into Vietnam

Vietnam has rapidly emerged as one of Asia’s most important manufacturing and industrial investment destinations.

Over the past decade, the country has attracted increasing foreign direct investment (FDI) from:

  • South Korea,
  • Japan,
  • Singapore,
  • Taiwan,
  • China,
  • the United States,
  • and European industrial groups.

This growth has been driven by several structural factors:

  • China+1 manufacturing strategies,
  • supply chain diversification,
  • competitive operating costs,
  • export-oriented industrial policy,
  • political stability,
  • and Vietnam’s integration into global trade agreements.

For many international manufacturers, factory setup Vietnam is no longer merely a cost-saving decision.

It is increasingly viewed as:

  • a strategic industrial positioning move,
  • a Southeast Asia expansion platform,
  • and a long-term manufacturing diversification strategy.

Legal Framework for Factory Setup Vietnam

Factory setup in Vietnam is not limited to company incorporation. A foreign manufacturing project normally requires coordinated review of investment approval, enterprise registration, industrial land or factory lease, environmental obligations, construction and fire safety requirements, labour compliance, customs readiness, tax registration and post-licensing implementation.

The legal framework should be assessed under the current Law on Investment, Law on Enterprises, environmental, construction, fire safety, labour, tax, customs and sector-specific regulations, as amended and implemented from time to time. Because manufacturing projects are implemented locally, provincial practice and industrial zone requirements remain important even where the national legal framework is the same.

Foreign manufacturers should not rely only on general incorporation timelines. The practical schedule depends on the project scope, location, industrial zone readiness, environmental classification, construction plan, machinery import requirements, workforce plan and authority review process. A legally complete factory setup roadmap should therefore cover both licensing and operational readiness.

The IRC records:

  • project objectives,
  • investment capital,
  • operational term,
  • project location,
  • investor information,
  • and manufacturing activities.

For many foreign manufacturers, obtaining the IRC is the first major legal milestone.

The approval process often requires assessment of:

  • project feasibility,
  • industrial zoning compatibility,
  • environmental considerations,
  • and investment conditions.

Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC)

After obtaining the IRC, investors generally proceed with incorporation of the Vietnamese legal entity through the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC).

The ERC establishes:

  • the company,
  • enterprise code,
  • tax registration,
  • legal representative,
  • and corporate existence.

For most manufacturing projects, both:

  • IRC,
    and:
  • ERC
    are required before operational implementation can begin.

Industrial Park Selection and Location Due Diligence

One of the most underestimated stages of factory setup Vietnam is industrial location due diligence.

Not all industrial zones are suitable for:

  • heavy manufacturing,
  • electronics production,
  • semiconductor operations,
  • industrial coating,
  • or environmentally intensive manufacturing.

Foreign investors typically evaluate:

  • industrial zoning,
  • infrastructure quality,
  • electricity stability,
  • wastewater treatment systems,
  • labor availability,
  • logistics connectivity,
  • and provincial licensing efficiency.

Location selection directly affects:

  • environmental approvals,
  • operational scalability,
  • customs efficiency,
  • and long-term investment performance.

Land Lease Structure for Manufacturing Projects

Foreign investors generally do not own industrial land directly in Vietnam.

Instead, manufacturing companies typically operate through:

  • industrial land leases,
  • ready-built factories,
  • build-to-suit facilities,
  • or industrial sublease structures.

Investors must carefully review:

  • land use rights,
  • lease terms,
  • infrastructure obligations,
  • permitted industrial activities,
  • and environmental conditions.

Land due diligence remains one of the most critical legal stages in manufacturing investment projects.

Environmental Permits for Factory Setup Vietnam

Environmental compliance has become increasingly important as Vietnam moves up the industrial value chain.

Depending on the manufacturing sector, projects may require:

  • Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA),
  • environmental licenses,
  • wastewater approvals,
  • hazardous waste registration,
  • emissions compliance,
  • and environmental monitoring systems.

This is especially relevant for:

  • semiconductor manufacturing,
  • electronics production,
  • metal processing,
  • industrial coating,
  • chemical operations,
  • and CNC manufacturing.

Environmental licensing delays are among the most common bottlenecks in factory implementation projects.

Construction Licensing and Factory Development

Factories involving new construction often require:

  • construction permits,
  • technical design approvals,
  • fire prevention approvals,
  • completion inspections,
  • and operational acceptance procedures.

This stage typically requires coordination among:

  • construction authorities,
  • industrial park management boards,
  • environmental agencies,
  • and fire prevention authorities.

Construction licensing directly impacts:

  • project timelines,
  • machinery installation schedules,
  • and manufacturing launch capability.

Labor Compliance for Manufacturing Companies

Manufacturing projects in Vietnam must comply with:

  • labor registration,
  • employment contracts,
  • payroll regulations,
  • social insurance,
  • occupational safety,
  • and working hour regulations.

Factories employing foreign experts may additionally require:

  • work permits,
  • visas,
  • temporary residence cards,
  • and foreign labor approvals.

Labor compliance becomes increasingly important for:

  • high-tech manufacturing,
  • semiconductor operations,
  • automation engineering,
  • and industrial production environments.

Tax Incentives for Manufacturing Investment

Vietnam provides various tax incentives for:

  • manufacturing projects,
  • high-tech industries,
  • export-oriented operations,
  • semiconductor investment,
  • industrial R&D,
  • and investment in prioritized sectors or locations.

Potential incentives may include:

  • Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reductions,
  • tax holidays,
  • import duty exemptions,
  • land rental incentives,
  • and accelerated depreciation mechanisms.

However, incentive eligibility depends heavily on:

  • project structure,
  • sector classification,
  • investment scale,
  • and geographic location.

Customs and Import Procedures

Manufacturing companies importing:

  • machinery,
  • production lines,
  • raw materials,
  • or industrial equipment
    must complete customs and import procedures.

This often includes:

  • customs code registration,
  • VNACCS activation,
  • import duty classification,
  • and customs compliance systems.

Export-oriented manufacturers additionally evaluate:

  • bonded warehouse structures,
  • export processing regimes,
  • and tariff optimization strategies.

Customs readiness is essential for operational continuity.

Post-Licensing Compliance: The Most Overlooked Stage

Many investors mistakenly believe factory setup Vietnam is completed after incorporation.

Incorporation is where the execution phase begins, not where the project ends. In reality, post-licensing procedures often determine whether a factory can legally operate.

This stage may involve:

  • tax registration,
  • VAT activation,
  • e-invoice registration,
  • social insurance setup,
  • customs activation,
  • bank account opening,
  • capital contribution,
  • and operational licensing.

Without proper post-licensing execution, factories may be unable to:

  • issue invoices,
  • recruit employees,
  • import machinery,
  • or commence production.

FAQ: Factory Setup Vietnam

How long does factory setup Vietnam usually take?

Depending on project complexity, factory setup Vietnam may take several months due to:

  • investment licensing,
  • environmental approvals,
  • construction procedures,
  • customs activation,
  • and operational compliance requirements.

Can foreigners fully own manufacturing companies in Vietnam?

Yes.

Many manufacturing sectors in Vietnam allow:

  • 100% foreign ownership,
    subject to:
  • investment regulations,
  • sector-specific conditions,
  • and licensing requirements.

What are the most important licenses for factory setup Vietnam?

Typical approvals include:

  • Investment Registration Certificate (IRC),
  • Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC),
  • environmental permits,
  • construction permits,
  • fire prevention approvals,
  • customs registration,
  • and tax activation.
