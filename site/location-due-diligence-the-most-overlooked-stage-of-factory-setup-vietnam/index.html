Location Due Diligence: The Most Overlooked Stage of Factory Setup Vietnam

Summary

Location due diligence is one of the most underestimated yet strategically important stages of factory setup Vietnam. Many foreign manufacturers focus heavily on labor costs, tax incentives, and factory construction, while overlooking zoning risks, infrastructure limitations, environmental constraints, logistics connectivity, labor availability, and provincial licensing practices. In practice, factory location selection often determines the long-term operational success, compliance stability, and scalability of manufacturing investment projects in Vietnam.

Why Location Selection Has Become a Strategic Issue in Factory Setup Vietnam

Vietnam has become one of Asia’s fastest-growing manufacturing destinations for:

  • electronics manufacturing,
  • semiconductor supply chains,
  • CNC machining,
  • industrial automation,
  • automotive suppliers,
  • and export-oriented industrial production.

International investors are increasingly implementing:

  • China+1 strategies,
  • regional supply chain diversification,
  • Southeast Asia manufacturing expansion,
  • and long-term industrial repositioning.

However, many investors continue to underestimate one critical factor:

Factory setup Vietnam is heavily dependent on location strategy.

The difference between:

  • a well-positioned industrial project,
    and:
  • a problematic factory investment,
    often begins with location due diligence.

In Vietnam, factory location affects:

  • licensing feasibility,
  • environmental approvals,
  • labor recruitment,
  • customs efficiency,
  • logistics costs,
  • infrastructure reliability,
  • and future operational scalability.

The Common Misconception About Industrial Land in Vietnam

A common misconception among foreign investors is that:
industrial land is largely interchangeable.

In reality, industrial zones in Vietnam differ significantly in:

  • licensing readiness,
  • infrastructure quality,
  • environmental restrictions,
  • power supply stability,
  • wastewater treatment capability,
  • labor availability,
  • logistics connectivity,
  • and local government implementation efficiency.

A factory location that appears commercially attractive may later face:

  • environmental licensing barriers,
  • construction delays,
  • labor shortages,
  • customs bottlenecks,
  • or infrastructure limitations.

For many manufacturing projects, location mistakes become long-term operational liabilities.

Why Location Due Diligence Is More Than Site Selection

Location due diligence is not simply about:

  • finding available industrial land,
    or:
  • negotiating lease costs.

It is a multidisciplinary assessment involving:

  • legal feasibility,
  • industrial zoning,
  • environmental compliance,
  • operational logistics,
  • labor economics,
  • infrastructure capacity,
  • and provincial regulatory practice.

For foreign manufacturers, location due diligence often determines:

  • whether the investment can be licensed efficiently,
  • whether the factory can scale,
  • and whether the operation remains commercially sustainable.

Core Location Due Diligence Factors in Factory Setup Vietnam

Before finalizing industrial land selection, investors typically evaluate multiple operational and regulatory factors.

1. Industrial Zoning Compatibility

One of the first due diligence questions is whether the project is legally compatible with:

  • provincial planning,
  • industrial zoning,
  • and land-use classification.

Not all industrial zones permit:

  • heavy manufacturing,
  • chemical processing,
  • electronics production,
  • semiconductor activities,
  • or environmentally intensive operations.

Incorrect zoning assumptions can delay:

  • Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) approval,
  • environmental licensing,
  • and factory construction.

2. Infrastructure Capacity and Utility Stability

Infrastructure reliability has become increasingly important for:

  • semiconductor manufacturing,
  • electronics production,
  • CNC machining,
  • and industrial automation operations.

Investors typically assess:

  • electricity stability,
  • backup power systems,
  • water supply capacity,
  • wastewater treatment infrastructure,
  • telecommunications connectivity,
  • and transportation access.

For high-tech manufacturing, unstable infrastructure can directly affect:

  • production continuity,
  • equipment reliability,
  • and operational efficiency.

3. Environmental Due Diligence

Environmental compliance is one of the largest hidden risks in factory setup Vietnam.

Different industrial parks have different:

  • environmental thresholds,
  • wastewater treatment capabilities,
  • hazardous waste restrictions,
  • and emissions capacity.

Projects involving:

  • metal processing,
  • industrial coating,
  • semiconductor packaging,
  • electronics manufacturing,
  • or chemical usage
    may face stricter environmental scrutiny.

Environmental incompatibility can significantly delay:

  • Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA),
  • environmental licensing,
  • and operational approvals.

4. Logistics and Supply Chain Connectivity

Factory location directly affects:

  • import-export efficiency,
  • shipping costs,
  • customs operations,
  • and supply chain responsiveness.

Manufacturing investors often evaluate proximity to:

  • seaports,
  • airports,
  • expressways,
  • logistics hubs,
  • and supplier ecosystems.

This is particularly important for:

  • export-oriented manufacturing,
  • semiconductor supply chains,
  • electronics production,
  • and time-sensitive industrial operations.

In many cases, logistics inefficiencies create larger long-term costs than land pricing itself.

5. Labor Market and Workforce Availability

Labor availability differs significantly across Vietnam.

Some industrial provinces face:

  • labor shortages,
  • rising wage pressure,
  • high employee turnover,
  • and increasing competition for technical workers.

Investors increasingly evaluate:

  • local workforce size,
  • engineering talent availability,
  • vocational training ecosystems,
  • labor mobility,
  • and housing infrastructure for employees.

This becomes critically important for:

  • semiconductor operations,
  • electronics manufacturing,
  • automation engineering,
  • and precision industrial production.

6. Provincial Regulatory and Licensing Practice

Provincial implementation capability remains one of the least discussed aspects of factory setup Vietnam.

Different provinces often demonstrate different:

  • licensing efficiency,
  • administrative responsiveness,
  • infrastructure readiness,
  • and operational support capability.

In practice, provincial execution quality can significantly affect:

  • project timelines,
  • permit approvals,
  • construction schedules,
  • and manufacturing launch speed.

For international manufacturers, local implementation capability often matters as much as national policy.

Why Industrial Park Selection Has Become More Strategic

Vietnam’s industrial ecosystem is becoming increasingly specialized.

Certain industrial zones increasingly focus on:

  • electronics manufacturing,
  • semiconductor supply chains,
  • automotive suppliers,
  • precision engineering,
  • logistics,
  • or export-oriented manufacturing.

As Vietnam moves up the industrial value chain, industrial clustering becomes more important.

Manufacturers increasingly benefit from proximity to:

  • supplier ecosystems,
  • engineering talent pools,
  • logistics infrastructure,
  • and supporting industries.

Location due diligence is therefore increasingly linked to long-term industrial competitiveness.

Common Location Due Diligence Risks for Foreign Investors

Foreign manufacturers commonly encounter issues involving:

  • unsuitable zoning,
  • hidden infrastructure limitations,
  • environmental incompatibility,
  • labor shortages,
  • weak logistics connectivity,
  • licensing delays,
  • and unrealistic industrial park commitments.

These risks can delay:

  • factory construction,
  • machinery installation,
  • operational approvals,
  • production launch,
  • and customer onboarding.

In many cases, correcting a poor location decision becomes extremely costly after investment implementation begins.

Why Vietnam Continues to Attract Manufacturing Investment

Despite increasing operational complexity, Vietnam remains highly attractive for global manufacturers because of:

  • strategic geographic positioning,
  • industrial scalability,
  • export-oriented growth,
  • competitive operating costs,
  • engineering workforce development,
  • and integration into global supply chains.

Vietnam increasingly plays a strategic role in:

  • semiconductor ecosystems,
  • electronics manufacturing,
  • industrial automation,
  • precision engineering,
  • and Southeast Asian manufacturing diversification.

For many international investors, factory setup Vietnam is no longer simply about cost reduction.

It is about building long-term industrial positioning within Asia’s evolving manufacturing ecosystem.
