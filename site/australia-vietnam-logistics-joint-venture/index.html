Why Australian Logistics Companies Are Expanding into Vietnam

Vietnam has become a strategic logistics hub within Asia-Pacific, supported by:

  • continued growth in foreign direct investment;
  • expansion of export-oriented manufacturing;
  • modern seaport and airport infrastructure;
  • increasing demand for integrated logistics services;
  • deeper regional trade integration.

Many Australian logistics companies already support customers manufacturing in Vietnam. Establishing a local presence enables closer customer relationships, better operational control and broader service offerings.

For many investors, a joint venture with an experienced Vietnamese logistics company offers a practical route to market while combining international capabilities with local market knowledge.

The Business Challenge

The Australian investor planned to establish a long-term presence in Vietnam through a joint venture with an existing Vietnamese logistics operator.

The parties intended to combine:

  • international freight forwarding expertise;
  • local operational capabilities;
  • established customer relationships;
  • regional business development;
  • integrated logistics solutions.

However, before proceeding, several legal and commercial issues required careful planning. These included:

  • foreign investment conditions;
  • ownership structure;
  • governance arrangements;
  • reserved matters;
  • management appointments;
  • capital contributions;
  • exit mechanisms;
  • compliance with Vietnam’s logistics regulations.

Why Joint Ventures Require More Than Company Incorporation

A successful joint venture is built on governance rather than registration alone. While incorporation establishes the legal entity, the long-term success of the business depends on clear agreements between the shareholders.

Typical issues include:

  • decision-making authority;
  • board composition;
  • appointment of directors;
  • reserved matters requiring unanimous approval;
  • dividend policy;
  • transfer restrictions;
  • deadlock resolution;
  • dispute resolution mechanisms.

Addressing these issues at the outset significantly reduces future shareholder conflicts.

Designing a Sustainable Joint Venture

Rather than focusing solely on incorporation, the project prioritised governance mechanisms capable of supporting long-term cooperation. Particular attention was given to:

Governance Framework

Clearly defining decision-making authority between shareholders.

Management Structure

Balancing operational autonomy with shareholder oversight.

Capital Commitments

Establishing transparent funding obligations and future capital raising procedures.

Exit Planning

Providing mechanisms for future share transfers, business expansion or orderly separation if commercial objectives changed.

Common Risks in Cross-Border Joint Ventures

Foreign investors frequently underestimate the governance challenges associated with joint ventures. Common issues include:

Misaligned Expectations

Commercial objectives should be discussed before legal drafting begins.

Inadequate Shareholders’ Agreements

Generic templates rarely address the practical realities of operating a logistics business in Vietnam.

Unclear Reserved Matters

Failure to define matters requiring shareholder approval can delay operational decisions or create unnecessary disputes.

Governance Drift

As the business grows, undocumented practices may gradually replace agreed governance procedures. Maintaining proper corporate governance protects both shareholders and management.

Practical Lessons for Foreign Investors

1. Select the Right Partner

Successful joint ventures depend on strategic alignment as much as financial capability.

2. Invest Time in Governance

Well-designed governance arrangements often prevent disputes years later.

3. Consider Regulatory Requirements Early

Vietnam’s logistics sector includes investment conditions that should be incorporated into transaction planning from the outset.

4. Balance Flexibility with Control

Governance documents should support business growth without creating unnecessary administrative burdens.

5. Plan Beyond Day One

A joint venture should include mechanisms for future expansion, investment and succession—not simply company establishment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreign investors establish a logistics company in Vietnam?

Yes. Depending on the logistics services involved, foreign investment may be permitted subject to Vietnam’s international commitments and sector-specific regulations.

Why choose a joint venture instead of a wholly foreign-owned company?

A joint venture may provide access to local operational expertise, established customer relationships and market knowledge while sharing commercial risks.

Is a Shareholders’ Agreement mandatory?

Vietnamese law does not require one, but it is strongly recommended to regulate governance, shareholder rights and dispute resolution.

What legal issues should be reviewed before establishing a joint venture?

Typical considerations include foreign investment conditions, governance structure, management authority, capital contributions, intellectual property, competition issues and exit mechanisms.
